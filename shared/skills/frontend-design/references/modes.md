# Visitor modes

Includes modified guidance from Impeccable and Anthropic. See [sources](SOURCES.md).

Choose the mode by what success looks like to a visitor of this surface. A tool's landing page persuades; its dashboard operates; its docs index reads. Record the mode in the surface brief, not as a property of the product. Native navigation, input, and accessibility conventions hold across all four modes.

## Operate

Start with the task, current state, and the next action. Use a clear workspace, dependable navigation, and secondary context such as an inspector. Standard tabs, sidebars, breadcrumbs, search, and keyboard shortcuts are useful when they match what users expect. One workhorse family, a fixed rem role scale, a second neutral surface for tools, one active accent, and density matched to frequency carry more brand than ornament. Prefer inline changes over modals. Use product-native controls, consistent hit areas and state vocabulary, and task-specific empty states. Use cached content or stable skeletons rather than a spinner replacing the workspace. Keep overlays outside clipping ancestors.

In an expressive world, borrow type, palette, density, print or screen notation, and at most one signature idea. Keep standard interaction affordances. Do not dress payment UI as a cash register or a production workspace as a scope. Show the core working region, credible rows or figures, and an action in the first viewport. In comps, change scaffolding, hierarchy, workspace, or information density; do not change the identity or replace actual controls with illustration. A regular user's trust and efficiency outrank applause.

## Read

Answer the reader's question or frame it in the opening. Show current location, a reading column, headings or contents, and the way forward. Keep navigation, figures, notes, code, and tables in familiar forms. Start with body near 16px and a 60–75 character column, then fit the type and language. Give long prose a calm rhythm and high contrast; save mono for code, parameters, and compact labels. Useful influences include field guides, type specimens, technical manuals, timetables, newspaper wayfinding, and real publishing apparatus. Let the world own the masthead, rail, running head, margins, or treatment of code, not swallow the column. For comps, show real title, opening and hardest content; vary wayfinding or the margins instead of doing violence to measure. Cover the logo and ask whether the frame still belongs to this product.

## Persuade

Make the visitor understand the offer, see why to care, and act. Lead with the mechanism, object, place, or proof only this product can supply. Prefer a real working form when it is the action people came for, such as route and date selection or available appointments. Do not substitute a vague link when the working affordance belongs here. A useful narrative is identity and offer, a concrete proof point or supporting image, deeper context or workflow, credible evidence, then a final action. Give each section one purpose and one dominant idea. One H1, at most six sections, two faces, and one primary CTA group are useful initial prompt constraints, not quotas.

Compose the first viewport as one scene, not a dashboard; prefer the actual brand/product/place/person name or literal offer in H1, and put the benefit in the support sentence rather than a slogan louder than the brand. Hide the nav to test recognition. A compelling image or interactive full-canvas plane may go edge to edge; by default do not frame it as a card, tile collage, or two unrelated halves. Constrain inner content only. Use a calm text zone; make type roughly two to three headline lines at desktop and glance-readable on mobile. Show brand, headline, short support, action group, and dominant visual. Leave secondary proof, metadata, schedules, badge clusters, and listings for below unless they are the task itself. Count sticky chrome in the viewport budget. Preserve a hint of the next section when that does not bury essentials. See [layout](layout-and-spacing.md) and [comps](imagery-and-comps.md).

## Experience

Put the work itself in the first viewport: gallery piece, live demo, interactive story, playable scene, or a portfolio artifact. Let the interface recede but remain discoverable, keyboard accessible, and predictable. Lead with an intentional entry point rather than a marketing intro or instructions wall. Respect playback and motion preferences. Give the visitor a way to progress, orient, pause, or leave. A medium can structure the entire surface; never flatten it to a decorative backdrop around vanilla sections.

## When the world drives the page

Persuade and Experience may translate materials, navigation topology, component states, and a signature interaction into the form the brief chose. Make links read as links and controls as controls inside that form. A book can structure a page, but a photographed cover with overlaid UI is not an interactive book; one book has one viewpoint, scale, and gutter, and more content can live on a subsequent spread. A painted storefront is not a site unless the parts work where placed. Use the world as the carrier of the page, not a picture of it. Avoid the opposite compromise, a neutral template with a brand-colored accent. In comps, make variants materially bolder or structurally different and keep one dominant move. Inspect the fold to verify the visitor can identify the mode without a caption.
