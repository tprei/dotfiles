# Imagery and comps

Includes modified guidance from Impeccable. See [sources](SOURCES.md).

## Use an image with a job

Show the actual product, person, place, use, or gameplay when inspection is the point. Prefer approved and supplied assets. OpenAI's visual blog prefers supplied or generated assets over web links unless requested; its prompt guide and Impeccable allow search for relevant imagery. Use vetted, licensed sources when the brief and project permit, verify identity and resolution, and ship the licensed asset rather than a fragile external link. Search for the specific object, not the product category. A generated illustration may carry atmosphere, but never pass it off as a real site, result, person, or testimonial. When the user must supply a missing asset, describe its role and disclose the missing input. Do not silently substitute chrome.

Use crisp SVG for icons, authentic logos, authored linework, diagrams and charts, not a primitive-built stand-in for photographic form. For engraving in vector, build form with line and tone with purposeful clipped hatching, not a flat fill in a silhouette. Use a finished raster for photographic, dimensional, textured, or painted material. Respect real alpha and intentional crop; check legibility against both themes. Crop out distracting signage and use an even tonal area when text overlays imaging. If the object must be inspected, do not hide it with darkening, blur, or a heavy wash. Use separate assets for separate moments rather than a prefab collage with fake UI. Keep UI text and controls semantic over the image. Give each raster its origin and permission or exact generation provenance in a durable asset record; remove abandoned assets. See [optimize](optimize.md) for delivery.

Keep exact generation prompts in development-only asset records when they contain working instructions or context. Ship no hidden prompt text. For isolated plates, inspect real alpha rather than chroma-keying a painted checkerboard; an empty opening in a frame must have no baked view or halo.

## Explore composition without changing the identity

For a comp-led surface, use three genuine high-fidelity compositions at the target aspect and pixel size. Treat a decision comp as option one when it exists; do not regenerate it. Compare the lead, topology, sequence, density, hierarchy, and interaction framing. Variants must be distinguishable as thumbnails. Keep palette, typographic voice, materials, and motion grammar fixed. Let a representative screenshot carry established brand style rather than re-describing it in prose. Do not feed comp one back as a visual reference for two near-duplicates. Use existing assets and reference boards to calibrate quality and material, not to copy their page layout.

When directing a render, use roughly 150–300 words: name this surface, visitor job, real subject, key content and units, next action, structural lead and reading order, and real edge-to-edge frame, navigation, utilities, or their absence. No browser or device surround. Name which parts the source image contributes; without a source image, use two to four sentences of concrete visual rules. Do not stipulate every position or style. Include the beginning of a following section if it proves the system holds beyond the opening. Reject a poster, stock photo with text, interchangeable template, absent subject, two competing foci, or a view whose mode cannot be identified without a caption. Show the three together for approval, or record the explicitly delegated choice. A mood board is not an approved layout.

## Measure the approved comp

1. Keep the original unchanged. Inventory every salient region, including layers and the signature interaction: bounding box, overlap, ink and ground, size, cap height, width class, weight, wrap, and reading order. A region larger than one quarter of the comp is usually a band, not one control or text element. Narrow a text or control crop only if it retains at least 95% of the contrasting ink. Inspect exact crops, masked overlaps and excluded pixels; empty or clipped crops indicate wrong geometry. Do not let large code boxes mask real artwork between labels. Distinguish a containing frame from its children.
2. Split by what varies independently: lettered labels, buttons, real-time diagrams, frames, views, filters, media, and moving parts. For a scene behind shutters, give the frame a transparent opening, give the view its own responsive image, and each shutter its own moving layer. Painted annotation lines belong with the drawing; live labels are text. Do not add decorative rules, kickers, nav items, or ink blocks just to fill space. Match lettering by visible cap height, compression, weight, and terminals; select the closest licensable face and serve it with consistent metrics.
3. Produce plates before assembling. A plate is a production asset that matches the crop's subject, silhouette, composition, lighting, texture, camera, and color, minus live text, rounded UI clipping, and chrome. Target at least 1.5× the region dimensions. A comp crop is evidence, not a shipping asset. Use genuine alpha for isolated objects or ink; preserve white paint, cutouts and fine edges, and inspect over light and dark. Use opaque photos or textures where appropriate; a clean texture patch can be mirror-tiled when seams stay invisible. Use approved local assets. A single-file format does not excuse using a comp screenshot instead of separate assets.
4. Show the production region plan and plates for a substantive comp-led build before implementing the first viewport. Keep the signed-off frame and revise only affected regions based on feedback. A missing decision is not approval. Place assets at measured boxes first, then semantic text and controls, at the comp's own size and with the approved copy. Verify presence, object fit, ground temperature, alignment, letterforms, and contrast crop by crop. Separate **missing** material, **contradicted** structure, and **drift** in size or spacing; fix the type of discrepancy actually present. A numerically close render with wrong material, an invented element, or a clipped plate is not faithful.
5. After the core viewport holds, extend the same system below it, add one signature sequence, and adapt without forfeiting the focal arrangement. Inspect desktop across roughly 1280–1600px, capture a 1440px desktop and 390px phone when relevant, and compare at the approved comp's exact dimensions too. An element pushed out of the intended fold is displaced; resize or restructure what pushes it. If you miss the intended opening three times, review the exact evidence with the user rather than ratcheting small changes blindly. Honor the accepted result, but keep truth, presence, input semantics, and accessibility non-negotiable.

Fidelity is to composition, type character, medium, foreground and ground, working hierarchy, and behavior. It is not a license to rasterize a UI or carry desktop coordinates unmodified to mobile. A code-led reference only sets the quality bar unless approved as a comp. See [pre-ship review](pre-ship.md).
